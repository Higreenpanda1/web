# Weekly blog — 10 October 2026

## What the market says this week

- **Hormuz is still closed to most commercial shipping.** It has been since 28 February 2026. Iraq has been hit hardest: the MSC Sariska V was struck at Umm Qasr on 1 June, and Iraqi cargo now moves mainly through Jordan's Aqaba port and then overland (Channel8, 31 May 2026). Saudi and Gulf routing was covered in the articles published 29 September and 6 October.
- **Asia–Europe container rates have fallen for 12 weeks in a row.** Drewry's WCI on 1 October 2026 put Shanghai–Rotterdam at USD 3,399 per 40ft and Shanghai–Genoa at USD 3,702. More ships are going through Suez again: Maersk and Hapag-Lloyd will route their AE12 service through the canal in both directions from 27 October 2026 (Ahram Online, 29 September 2026).
- **Europe: the €3 parcel duty is in force.** Since 1 July 2026 the EU charges a flat €3 per item on B2C parcels worth €150 or less. Product identifiers become mandatory from 1 November 2026, and a separate handling fee is under discussion for the end of 2026. The UK will remove its £135 relief by October 2028 at the latest (policy paper of 13 July, updated 30 September 2026). In 2024, 91% of EU parcels under €150 came from China.
- **Egypt: imports from China rose 14.3% to USD 10.4 billion in H1 2026** (CAPMAS). ACI pre-registration has been mandatory for air freight since 1 January 2026, as well as for sea freight. Egyptian importers keep asking about ACID, CargoX and the factory registration required under Decree 43.
- **Iraq: China supplied about USD 17 billion of goods in 2025** (Ministry of Trade). Since 15 September 2025, Iraq rejects electrical goods that arrive without the Iraqi Quality Mark, and COSQC updated its product standards list in June 2025.
