## What the market says (late October 2026)

- **Hormuz is still effectively closed, and getting more dangerous.**
  - Lloyd's List Intelligence (brief of 22 October 2026) counted at least 28 attacks in October, the highest monthly total since the war began.
  - UKMTO/JMIC (update of 15 October 2026) rates the threat severe.
  - Carriers send Gulf cargo through Jeddah, King Abdullah Port, Dammam, Salalah and Khor Fakkan. Hapag-Lloyd's rerouting charges are USD 3,000-5,500 per container for Qatar and Bahrain.
- **Suez is coming back.** The Suez Canal Authority reported 166 large container-ship transits in Q3 2026, against 16 a year earlier. On 21 October 2026 it said Hapag-Lloyd is gradually returning.
- **China-Europe rail is growing.** Q1 2026 saw 5,460 trips (+29%) and 546,000 TEU (+22%).
- **EU rules land in December and the months after.**
  - The EUDR applies from 30 December 2026 to large and medium operators (Regulation 2025/2650), and from 30 June 2027 to micro and small ones. The Commission classes China as low risk.
  - Under the Battery Regulation, battery passports and removable portable batteries start on 18 February 2027, and supply-chain due diligence on 18 August 2027.
  - The German packaging law (VerpackDG) and France's IDU numbers keep tightening control of marketplace sellers.
- **Visa-free entry.** UK and 25 EU passports enter China visa-free until 31 December 2026; no 2027 extension had been announced as of 26 October. Visa-free entries reached nearly 9 million in Q3 2026.
- **The trade calendar.**
  - Chinese New Year falls on 6 February 2027, and Ramadan is expected around 8 February 2027.
  - Stock for Ramadan and for the spring season therefore has to ship before factories close in mid-January.

## Caveats the writers recorded (check before each publish date)

- **Visa-free entry (6 Dec).** If China announces a 2027 extension before 6 December, update the introduction, the extension section, the key takeaways and the first FAQ. The spring 2027 Canton Fair dates are labelled "expected".
- **Chinese New Year (29 Nov).** The State Council had not published the 2027 holiday schedule as of late October. If it appears in November, update the "not yet published" lines.
- **Battery Regulation (26 Nov).** The implementing act on the label format was still pending. The full label date is 18 August 2026 or 18 months after that act, whichever is later.
- **Hormuz figures** in the Qatar, Bahrain and Foshan articles are dated October 2026. Re-check them against the latest UKMTO note before they publish.
- **Bahrain.**
  - VAT is 10% (NBR). Bahrain Customs' FAQ still shows 5%, and the article flags this.
  - No official transit times were found, so none are given.
- **Lebanon.**
  - The customs dollar of LBP 89,500 comes from BLOMInvest (July 2025).
  - Whether the 3% fee still applies after 2024 is unverified.
  - customs.gov.lb could not be opened.
- **Tunisia.** The Central Bank circular 2026-4 (196 products that must be paid from the importer's own funds) comes from the Tunisian press, because the bct.gov.tn PDF was blocked.
- **Qatar.** customs.gov.qa was blocked, so the Al Nadeeb details come from Invest Qatar. The article hedges on VAT.
- **EPR.** The VerpackDG fine tiers and the 12 November 2026 deadline come from a law firm's summary. The Amazon dates come from Seller Central announcements.
- **EUDR.** The number of Delegated Regulation 2026/2102 and the 4-hectare polygon rule were not read in the Official Journal.
- **Supplier verification.** The credit-code prefixes (91/92/93) are worded "usually", and gsxt.gov.cn could not be opened.
- **Foshan.** No Saudi duty rate is given for Chapter 94, and no furniture-specific SASO regulation was found; the article sends readers to the ZATCA tariff tool. The E1/E0 limits come from the writer's knowledge.
- **Rail.** No freight rates are given. The Małaszewicze share is an industry estimate.
