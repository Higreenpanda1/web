# Weekly blog — 17 October 2026

## What the market says this week

- **Asia–Europe sea freight rose for the first time after 13 weeks of falls.** Drewry's WCI of 15 October 2026 put the composite at USD 4,485 per 40ft (+3%), Shanghai–Rotterdam at USD 3,522 (+6%) and Shanghai–Genoa at USD 3,945 (+7%). Carriers want FAK rates of USD 4,500–4,800 from 19 October. Drewry doubts they will hold, because Suez transits are adding capacity. Maersk moves four Gemini services (AE5, AE11, AE12 and ME2) back to Suez, with AE12 going through the canal both ways from 27 October 2026 (Maersk, 14 September 2026). Drewry reported Suez container transits in week 39 at 68% above 2025. The southern Red Sea is still a war-risk listed area for insurers.
- **Hormuz is still effectively closed.** IMF PortWatch showed about 1 commercial transit a day around 20 September, against about 85 before the crisis. On 8 October the IRGC said it had struck the LPG carrier NV Sunshine. Gulf rates stay high: SCFI Shanghai–Persian Gulf was USD 6,489 per TEU on 30 September. The Gulf routing was covered on 29 September and 6 October.
- **Jordan:** Aqaba Container Terminal handled 472,681 TEU in H1 2026. Transit import containers rose 155%, because Iraq-bound cargo now goes through Aqaba (Petra, 19 July 2026; Reuters/Zawya, 1 September 2026). The Amman Chamber of Commerce put imports from China at JD 4.18 billion in 2025. JSMO's Committed Trader programme had 151 companies in H1 2026.
- **Morocco:** goods imports were MAD 458.8 billion in H1 2026 (+15.3%) and the trade deficit MAD 198.4 billion (+23.5%) (Office des changes). China was Morocco's top supplier in 2025, with 13.9% of imports (French Treasury). Finance Law 50-25 changed several import duties from 1 January 2026, for example domestic freezers and small washing machines at 17.5%, PVC at 10% and jacquard knits at 30%. Morocco has no free trade agreement with China (Minister Mezzour, 12 June 2026).
- **Europe compliance:** the EU ETS covers 70% of shipping's 2025 emissions (surrendered in 2026) and 100% from 2027. The UK ETS covers only domestic maritime from 1 July 2026.
